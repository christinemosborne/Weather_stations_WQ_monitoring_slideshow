Our Framework

The Nine-Element
Watershed Plan

A nine-element watershed plan is an EPA-recognized, science-based roadmap for identifying water-quality problems, their sources, and the best strategies to fix them. The six new weather stations create a unique opportunity to build that foundation here.

🏛️

Utah Division of Water Quality

📡

USU & Navajo Utah Commission

🌿

Navajo Nation Chapters

The Challenge

Four Corners Water
Is Under Pressure

🌡️

Warming & Drought

Long-term station records confirm a strong warming trend driving higher evapotranspiration and declining river flows — a pattern Diné herders have witnessed for decades.

🌩️

Monsoon Extremes

Intense, short monsoon bursts cause flash floods, mobilize salts and sediment, and deliver sharp pollution pulses to streams and washes.

❄️

Shrinking Snowpack

Earlier, faster snowmelt — sped up by dust on snow and warmer air — compresses spring runoff, reducing dilution of salts and changing when clean water arrives.

💧

Springs at Risk

Desert springs in the area, including those near Hovenweep, show rising salinity and seasonal drying during multi-year droughts — affecting both people and range livestock.

The Station Network

The Navajo Utah Commission approved six stations — three in Utah, three south of the state line — funded by the Walton Family Foundation, Conscience Bay, Campbell Scientific, the Utah Colorado River Authority, and the U.S. Youth Climate Center.

Capturing localized precipitation and temperature patterns near the Utah–Arizona border.

📍

Hwy 191 / Aneth

📍

Navajo Mountain (×2)

Two stations to capture precipitation variability across elevations — essential for snowmelt forecasting.

📍

Oljato–Monument Valley

Sited near the cemetery for accessibility and open terrain, at chapter representatives' suggestion.

The Science Tools

What Each Station
Will Measure

Each station is a comprehensive environmental sensor — transmitting data to NOAA and national databases, making Navajo Utah communities visible in regional climate systems for the first time.

🌧️

Precipitation

Rain and snow measured with precision — linking every monsoon storm and snowfall event to downstream flow and water-quality responses.

🌡️

Temperature & Dew Point

Air temperature and dew point track evapotranspiration stress on range and help predict water temperature in nearby streams and springs.

☀️

Solar Radiation & UV

Solar and UV data support evaporation modeling and agricultural planning, and help interpret algal bloom risk in standing water.

🌱

Soil Temperature & Moisture

Real-time soil data guides rotational grazing decisions and helps identify when soils are too dry or saturated to support healthy range recovery.

Holistic Approach

Two Ways of Knowing —
Stronger Together

🌿 Diné Traditional Knowledge
  • Generations of observation of seasonal water availability
  • Knowledge of which plants indicate groundwater health
  • Memory of how range condition tracks rainfall and grazing cycles
  • Understanding of soil crust, erosion, and recovery patterns
  • Identification of historically healthy springs and seeps
  • Oral records of drought, flood, and climate cycles over lifetimes
📡 Weather Station Science
  • Continuous, time-stamped rainfall and temperature data
  • Real-time alerts when storm thresholds are exceeded
  • Precise water chemistry measurements across seasons
  • Long-term trend detection for warming and salinity change
  • Models that forecast runoff, erosion, and contamination risk
  • Shared data accessible to managers, agencies, and communities

Neither approach alone is sufficient. Together they create a monitoring system with both deep historical roots and real-time precision.

Nine-Element Watershed Plan

Supporting Regenerative
Agriculture on Navajo Land

Water-quality data gathered through the watershed plan — together with USU's weather stations and chapter input — will directly support projects that promote healthier soils, better grazing decisions, and more resilient food systems.

🐄

Grazing Management

Real-time soil moisture and range condition data — combined with traditional knowledge of grazing cycles — help time rotational grazing to protect soils and forage recovery.

🌱

Soil Health

Linking rainfall data with soil crust monitoring shows which areas are most vulnerable to erosion after monsoon flash floods and where restoration efforts are most needed.

🚰

Water Access & Safety

Knowing when springs are most stressed, or when wash water is contaminated after a monsoon, helps families and livestock avoid poor-quality water at the most critical times.

📅

Seasonal Planning

Long-term climate trends combined with traditional seasonal knowledge help chapters plan for droughts, adjust herd sizes, and protect infrastructure before extreme events.

Monitoring Design

How the Station Network
Works in Practice

📍

Co-Located Instruments

Rain and temperature gauges are placed alongside automatic loggers in washes and at river gauges — so every storm is matched to the exact water-quality response it triggers.

⚠️

Storm-Event Flagging

Weather data automatically flags storm events, alerting field crews when to prioritize water sampling in washes and main-stem rivers to capture contamination pulses.

📊

Hydrologic Context

Every water sample is tagged as "baseflow," "snowmelt," or "monsoon" — a framework that mirrors how traditional knowledge has always understood seasonal water differences.

🌿

Spring Monitoring

Springs are checked at climate markers — end of dry season, after wet winters, after multi-year drought — rather than at arbitrary calendar dates, matching the land's actual rhythms.

An Emerging Opportunity

Water Quality Monitoring —
A Natural Next Step

The six weather stations were designed for weather and grazing data — but UDWQ believes they represent a rare opportunity to add water quality monitoring that could transform the nine-element watershed plan.

🔗

Co-Located Sampling

Water quality sensors or sampling programs placed near station sites would link every precipitation event directly to a measurable water-quality response — the gold standard for watershed science.

📊

Storm-Event Context

The stations' real-time rainfall data would let UDWQ trigger targeted water sampling after monsoon events — capturing contamination pulses in washes and streams that are otherwise missed.

📡

NOAA Data Integration

Station data flowing to NOAA's national databases creates a shared platform where weather and water quality records can be analyzed together — strengthening the case for watershed plan projects.

💬

Community-Driven Priorities

UDWQ is not arriving with a pre-set monitoring plan — we want to hear from chapters about which waters matter most, what concerns exist, and how water quality data can best serve local needs.
